# Portfolio, roadmap, and refresh — stages 9–11

## 9. Construct the route and target portfolio

Apply [Goal calibration](goal-calibration.md) when constructing amounts and staffing. Cash and award commitments require separate totals.

Build a coherent portfolio from qualified opportunities and the organization's capacity. Balance renewals and new money, unrestricted and restricted support, relationships and applications, near-term sources and longer bets, markets, concentration, and fundraising effort. Keep debt/equity/subsidy capital in its own budget and pipeline.

When asked for advice, recommend a lead route, sequencing, and a conservative planning ramp with reasons. Use the benchmark as context, project/service budgets as need, and access/readiness as feasibility. Do not assume peer receipts are available to this organization or invent a probability-weighted forecast. If scenario probabilities are useful, label them as planning assumptions and explain the basis.

**Usable output:** a proposed channel portfolio showing purpose, target amount/range and basis, solicitation count, timing, owner or role, evidence dependencies, and what to defer. Distinguish proposed goals from user-approved goals. For proposed receipts, distinguish solicitation volume from expected awards and show a labeled conversion assumption or conditional scenario. Invitations and introductions must precede counting inaccessible funders toward a near-term plan. Reconcile per-channel totals to the overall plan, label annual versus multi-year funding, and avoid counting the same award in multiple lanes. A higher goal must have an explanation beyond adding peers.

## 10. Translate the strategy into an operating system

Convert chosen routes into an 8–12 week plan and a twelve-month sequence. For each workstream, show action, owner, due week/date, dependency, desired outcome, and output. Use the user's named people and workload constraints; if owners are unknown, propose roles and mark assignments pending. Do not invent staffing or access.

The plan should cover the first conversation, purpose of the ask, materials, follow-up, application/sign-off when appropriate, and stewardship/reporting. Separate immediate tasks from public-source research, private inputs, and longer evidence-building work. Draft requested outreach or request packets as artifacts; composing an email does not authorize sending it.

Use simple development systems that suit the team: a pipeline table with prospect/program, ask/instrument, stage, next step, owner, deadline, source/check date, and follow-up; a regular review cadence; acknowledgment and stewardship responsibilities; and a phased materials/evidence build. Show readiness-to-action links: one-pager and project pipeline first, outcome dashboard and cases next, a deeper evaluation only where it serves the case.

**Usable output:** an owned first-weeks plan, phased materials checklist, prospect/account plans, and proposed review cadence. A plan with missing assignments can be a useful working draft; state what leadership must decide before execution.

### Packaging a strategy or board memo

When the user asks for a final strategy, integrate the work into a readable artifact instead of returning another research menu. A useful structure is:

1. Executive recommendation and the decision requested.
2. Organization position and sourced peer benchmark, with comparability caveats.
3. Chosen case, route mix, markets, and staged goals with their basis.
4. Qualified opportunities and access/readiness decisions.
5. Near-term roadmap with owners, materials, timing, and channel goals.
6. Longer program/pilot bets, evidence and replication requirements.
7. Development systems, responsibilities, sources, gaps, and refresh triggers.

Adapt the length and format to the audience. Charts and tables should survive being copied into another document: include metric/year/scope, source, and key caveat in the visual itself. Name the public program or source page in citations, rather than using a private warehouse table as the sole reference. Source date and inspection date are distinct. Use the host assistant's available document tools to create files when requested; do not claim files or hosting were created if they were not.

Call the result a working strategy, decision draft, or reviewed roadmap according to the evidence and decisions actually present. Do not label it board-ready while contacts, dates, amounts, material claims, or required decisions remain unreviewed. Useful proposals can remain in the draft with clear status.

## 11. Review, learn, and refresh

When the user adds peers, corrects a relationship, supplies a budget, rejects a route, or brings a new RFP, explain what changed, its evidence, which conclusions it affects, and which decisions remain in force. Recompute the affected comparisons; do not merely append an organization to a list. New developer peers can change a services-only diagnosis without necessarily changing the dollar goal. A representative older filing may still be useful, but its reason and year must remain visible.

Expiry, declined awards, capacity changes, outcomes, and new source evidence can trigger a route/account update. Preserve user edits and accepted reasoning, propose the specific revision, and avoid rewriting unrelated sections. Duplicate or immaterial evidence can warrant no change.

**Usable output:** a dated revision note with change, source, affected diagnosis/goal/route/tasks, retained decisions, open decisions, and next review trigger. For an end-to-end refresh, return the updated strategy artifact as well as the change note. Propose a cadence when useful; do not claim scheduling or durable strategy storage exists. For explicitly requested public-source saving follow [connection boundaries](../../azimuth-research/references/connection-boundaries.md); strategic choices stay in the user-visible artifact/conversation.
