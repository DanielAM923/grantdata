# Evidence, case, and opportunities — stages 5–8

## 5. Build project analogs and the evidence base

Turn the user's idea into a testable question: population, setting, delivery model, staff roles, costs, intended outcomes, and replication value. Find direct and adjacent analogs through Azimuth source leads and the host assistant's native research. Inspect original program pages, reports, evaluations, and payer announcements. An organization doing related work does not establish that it runs the same model, that the model works, or that a named funder paid for it.

For each analog, describe the actual model, direct/adjacent fit, results, study strength, costs when available, payer evidence, transfer conditions, and limitations. Cite the underlying source and locator; write a readable case study instead of pasting raw chunks. Keep “payer not found after this search” distinct from “not investigated.” Unknown payer evidence does not establish an unfunded program.

**Usable output:** an analog/evidence matrix plus a pilot concept: learning question, implementation plan, partner requirements, budget basis, baseline and outcome measures, and replication deliverable. For staffing/service pilots, address competencies, hiring/training, supervision, escalation, resident experience, workload, and appropriate safeguards. Do not promise causal benefits from an adjacent toolkit or descriptive case.

## 6. Design the case and funding architecture

Connect the need, the organization's assets, intervention, use of funds, outcomes, and evidence into a core case. Compare an operating/platform ask, a program ask, and a pilot/replication ask when relevant. Match each workstream to a suitable instrument: philanthropy, public grant/contract, bank donation, project subsidy, debt, guarantee, or equity. Separate grant funding from a housing capital stack; a grant edge alone cannot identify a financing instrument.

Design audience and market variants. A regional funder needs local eligibility and benefit evidence; an innovation funder needs a learning and adoption case. An existing bank partner may offer a useful introduction to its philanthropy or community-development desk, but a commercial relationship or CRA framework does not guarantee a gift or create an obligation to fund this applicant. Verify the specific bank channel.

**Usable output:** a case-and-route comparison with workstream, costs, ask type, evidence required, suitable sources, access needs, readiness, and tradeoffs. Recommend a lead route and sequence when asked, with a proposed planning ramp and explicit assumptions. Identify the user's choice or the unanswered input that would change that recommendation.

## 7. Build the opportunity universe

Start with the organization's current/lapsed supporters, selected peer funders, program payers, relevant public opportunities, capital providers, and verified relationship leads. `find_peer_funders` covers bounded historical grant observations for explicitly selected peers. Use separate calls for larger sets; distinguish different returned windows/caps and do not present a partial top list as a complete matrix. Do not infer “past three years” from an aggregate's latest year. Add native-search candidates when graph coverage is thin.

Classify the entity and the actual program: direct grantmaker, donor-advised sponsor, regrantor/system partner, operating partner, bank channel, or capital provider. Place candidates by market, workstream, and instrument. Record why each appeared, its source, and whether it is renewal, re-engagement, new, or unresolved. Keep candidates requiring verification available as such.

**Usable output:** a typed opportunity landscape or lane × market table with source, discovery rationale, coverage, and unknowns. Network paths explain possible relevance; they do not establish a warm introduction.

## 8. Qualify, reality-check, and prioritize

Inspect current funder-owned guidelines, program/application pages, RFPs, reports, and recent award announcements using the host assistant's native web search. Check the actual applicant, geography, activities, restrictions, instrument, amount basis, deadline/time zone, invitation or sponsor route, named contact/role, and materials required. Date the check. A live web page, active funder, and open relevant application are different findings.

Past application dates stay historical. Do not roll a recurring date into a later year or say a calendar is open without a published current cycle. Current amounts need program guidelines or comparable individual awards with purpose/year; multi-year totals, cohort sums, and deal values cannot be turned into a typical check size. Unknown contacts and amounts should remain explicit gaps.

Keep evidence confidence, relationship access, and strategic disposition separate. Prioritize outreach only when the route and case fit are supported; otherwise prioritize verification. Use dispositions such as pursue, learn/validate, steward, hold, or set aside, with reason, effort, dependency, and next step. A real user-supplied warm contact can inform priority; do not fabricate one from shared graph neighbors.

**Usable output:** an inspectable prospect/account table: lane and market; program/instrument; why it fits; amount and basis; current route/calendar and check date; contact or official channel; relationship provenance; readiness gap; proposed disposition and first conversation. Retain a verification queue for unresolved claims. A low-effort, well-fitting RFP can be useful, but a long list of open competitions does not replace a capacity-aware strategy.
