# Position, peers, and benchmark — stages 1–4

## 1. Define and normalize the ask

State the decision to support, focal organization or initiative, use of funds, audience, geography, horizon, and constraints. Carry forward the user's answers. A full strategy needs a useful initial brief; a live proposal can begin with its requirements. Give a provisional interpretation and proceed where it is safe to do so. Do not require a full intake questionnaire before first value.

**Usable output:** a short engagement brief with the decision, scope, known constraints, and materially missing inputs. A user correction changes the brief; do not silently reset it on later turns.

## 2. Establish current position and readiness

Resolve the legal entity and relevant initiative. Assess its revenue model, recent trajectory, concentration, fundraising history, service/development mix, project pipeline, outcomes, materials, and available people. Separate current financial evidence from public organizational descriptions and private facts the user supplied. Look for the actual applicant and budget: a parent nonprofit, property LLC, resident-services program, and development project can have different economics.

Use Azimuth identity and funding records, current official organization pages, primary filings, annual reports, and authorized user materials. Current and lapsed supporters, bank partners, board access, workload, and unrestricted needs may require user input. Public organizational names and NTEE codes alone cannot establish the service model.

**Usable output:** an organization frame and readiness diagnosis: what it can credibly ask for now, what needs preparation, and what is unknown. Recommend a comparison lens and an initial direction with assumptions, while retaining unresolved private inputs as requests.

## 3. Design and validate peer cohorts

Start with named peers and classify their purpose. Propose additional candidates from evidence about their activities, geography, scale, population, service intensity, ownership, and revenue model. A user-selected organization can remain a reference even when it is unsuitable for the financial cohort.

Use four lenses: operating/financial peers; reachable funding-market peers; program/model references; capital-stack/implementation neighbors. For a multi-market organization, distinguish local market-entry references from scale comparators. Check role coverage; a dense grant network can over-select service organizations and miss developers or neighborhood operators. Record accepted, proposed, set-aside, and reclassified peers with reasons and inference limits.

**Usable output:** a peer table with role, rationale, geographic/scale fit, source, and acceptance status. A cohort addition revisits both the financial diagnosis and the relevant funder discovery; explicitly state if only one was recomputed.

## 4. Benchmark and interpret the gap

Use the same metric, fiscal period, entity scope, and calculation across the accepted operating cohort. `compare_org_financials`, when available, returns selected organizations' filing metrics, source links, and coverage. Inspect linked primary filings or audited statements where missing fields, conflicting rows, transfers, or a changed conclusion matter. This tool does not select a fair cohort or prove a recurring fundraising capacity.

Keep these measures distinct:

- Total revenue and total contributions/grants.
- Non-government contributed support: contributions less reported government grants. This can include restricted, noncash, campaign, and affiliate support; it is not necessarily unrestricted recurring philanthropy.
- Related-organization contributions and any explicitly reviewed external-support adjustment. Explain whether the numerator, denominator, or both changed.
- Government grants, program/earned revenue, fundraising costs, and separately budgeted project capital.
- Historical incoming grant-edge totals, which can span years and never substitute for annual filing contributions.

Unknown fields stay unknown. When a sparse filing leaves a line blank, identify the convention and source check before treating it as zero. Do not equate the newest year with the most representative year. Inspect multi-year movement, campaign spikes, affiliate transfers, amendments/conflicting filings, short fiscal periods, and parent/property scope. Present outliers with reasons; do not discard inconvenient peers or blend distinct cohorts into a flattering average.

**Usable output:** a sourced comparison table and, when useful, a chart with fiscal year, metric definition, entity scope, and exclusions visible. Explain the gap and what could cause it. The comparison informs a proposed goal, not an automatic target.

Apply [Goal calibration](goal-calibration.md) before translating a benchmark into a numeric goal. When asked what the organization should aim for, show the arithmetic behind a planning range and a conservative ramp. Distinguish current receipts, new money, cumulative multi-year awards, annual run rate, and separate capital. Do not copy a past client's dollar ramp. Show what staffing, costs, project readiness, and service evidence make the proposed ramp plausible. If public evidence remains insufficient, produce a conditional scenario and the specific validation task; do not call it a verified benchmark.

A peer-ratio extrapolation is a scale-of-ambition scenario, not attainable twelve-month cash. Review affiliate, noncash, restricted and one-time receipts before interpreting contributions. With unknown staffing, access, renewals or pipeline, recommend the initial phase and a date for adopting a target. An explicit ask/conversion/payment illustration may help, but it is not a recommended target until access and capacity support its construction. Apply [Goal calibration](goal-calibration.md). Show benchmark ambition separately and keep service/developer cohorts distinct.
